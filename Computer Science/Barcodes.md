
Many items and packages are printed with barcodes for different purposes. Some are one-dimensional, like the UPC codes on the back of items on sale. Some are two-dimensional, like QR codes designed to help people quickly access information. 

## Formats

The form of a barcode is the conversion between the information it represents and visual code that can be scanned. There are many different formats, but the two most people are familiar with are UPC codes and QR codes.

### QR Codes

QR codes are two-dimensional codes that allow for many purposes, but are most commonly used to house a link to a web page. They are readable by most phone picture-taking applications. 

### UPC Codes

UPC codes are the common barcodes you see on most shoppable products. Usually around 13 digits, UPC codes are used for many things, but usually to identify a physical item.

#### ISBN Schema

One of the uses for UPC codes is the ISBN Schema. Books have a UPC code printed on the back, but they usually include a 5-digit 'addendum', or a smaller 5 code barcode alongside the main UPC code. The ISBN schema is used by books and defines the purposes of this: the main chunk of the UPC code is the number that represents that specific book. The addendum is usually included to supply recommended pricing information, and is not necessary to refer to a specific book.

#### Comic Book Schema

##### Phase One: 2-Digit Addendum

In the early days, Comic Books took the schema for their UPCs from Magazines. With the main chunk of the UPC, the code would signify which *title* we were referencing, then the 2-digit addendum would point to which issue we were referencing. 

##### Phase Two: 5-Digit Addendum

When comic books became collector items, some of the companies switched to a different schema. The main chunk of the 12-digit UPC still points to a specific title, but the addendum is 5-digits long. The first 3 digits of the addendum refer to which issue of the title. The next digit refers to the variant (comic books are released with 'variant covers', copies of the issue with different cover art for collectors), and the final refers to the printing.
